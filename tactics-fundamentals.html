Full contents

What the course covers

The workbook is organized into the parts below. Each part explains its ideas first, shows them in annotated examples, and then gives training positions with worked solutions.

  1. What makes a position tactical

    An opening section on the warning signs that tactics are available: undefended pieces, pieces lined up on the same file, rank or diagonal, and a king with few safe squares. These clues come up again in every chapter that follows.

  2. Forks

    Attacks on two targets with a single piece, starting with the knight and moving on to forks by pawns, bishops, rooks, queens and the king. Includes how a preliminary check or capture can set a fork up.

  3. Pins

    Absolute and relative pins, how to win material by adding pressure to a pinned piece, and how to break a pin when you are the one caught in it.

  4. Skewers

    The skewer as the reverse of a pin: a valuable piece is attacked and has to move, exposing the piece behind it. Covers skewers along files, ranks and diagonals, including skewers against the king.

  5. Discovered attacks

    Moving one piece to unmask an attack from another, including discovered checks and double checks, and why the piece that moves is so often free to cause damage of its own.

  6. Double attacks

    Threats against two targets at once using any combination of pieces, including moves that combine a mating threat with a threat to win material.

  7. Mixed training positions

    A closing set of positions where the theme is not announced in advance, so the first task is recognizing which idea fits. Every position has a fully worked solution.

Who it’s for

  • Players who know the rules and how the pieces move, and want a systematic introduction to tactics.
  • Players who often miss short tactics in their own games and want to study the patterns one at a time.
  • Adult learners and older juniors who prefer a written workbook they can study at their own board.

Who it isn’t for

  • Complete newcomers still learning how the pieces move. The course assumes you know the rules.
  • Experienced players looking for long, complex combinations. The positions here focus on clear ideas.
  • Anyone looking for video lessons or personal feedback. This is a self-study workbook.
