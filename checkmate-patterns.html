Full contents

What the course covers

The workbook is organized into the parts below. Each part explains its ideas first, shows them in annotated examples, and then gives training positions with worked solutions.

  1. Reading the king’s position

    How to count a king’s escape squares, see which of them are covered, and recognize when a single check would be decisive.

  2. Back-rank mates

    Mates against a king trapped behind its own pawns, the defensive resources that prevent them, and the tactics that remove a defender of the back rank.

  3. Mates with the queen

    Patterns where the queen works with the king, a knight or a bishop to cover the last escape squares, including mates on the edge of the board and in front of a castled king.

  4. Mates with rooks

    Two rooks working together, the rook and king mate, and rooks combining on the seventh and eighth ranks.

  5. Knight and bishop patterns

    Smothered mates, mates along the long diagonal, and patterns where the minor pieces work together against a cramped king.

  6. Opening the king

    Common sacrifices that remove pawn cover or draw the king into the open, with the mating pattern shown at the end of each line.

  7. Mixed training positions

    Positions where the pattern is not named in advance, so you have to recognize it first. Each worked solution covers the defender’s tries as well as the mate.

Who it’s for

  • Players who know the rules and want to recognize the standard mating patterns quickly.
  • Players who reach good attacking positions but are unsure how to finish them.
  • Anyone who wants a companion to Tactics Fundamentals with a sharper focus on the king.

Who it isn’t for

  • Complete newcomers still learning how the pieces move.
  • Players looking for a study of long attacking games or opening preparation.
  • Anyone looking for video lessons or personal feedback. This is a self-study workbook.
